### West Coast IPA: Rushmore IPA

*Source: [AHA - Rushmore IPA](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/rushmore-ipa-grain/) by John Palmer (Big Brew 2017)*

A classic West Coast IPA with just enough caramel and Munich malt to add complexity.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (18.93 L) |
| OG | 1.059 |
| FG | 1.015 |
| ABV | 6.0% |
| IBU | 70 |
| SRM | 6 |

**Grain Bill:**
- 11 lb (5 kg) Pale ale malt
- 1 lb (454 g) Munich malt
- 0.5 lb (227 g) Crystal 40°L

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| Nugget | 1.0 oz | 60 min | 13% |
| Cascade | 0.5 oz | 15 min | 6% |
| Amarillo | 0.5 oz | 15 min | 10% |
| Centennial | 0.5 oz | 15 min | 10.5% |
| Cascade | 0.5 oz | 0 min (whirlpool) | - |
| Amarillo | 0.5 oz | 0 min (whirlpool) | - |
| Centennial | 0.5 oz | 0 min (whirlpool) | - |
| Cascade | 0.5 oz | Dry hop (10 days) | - |
| Amarillo | 0.5 oz | Dry hop (10 days) | - |
| Centennial | 0.5 oz | Dry hop (10 days) | - |

**Yeast:** American ale yeast (US-05, WLP001, or Wyeast 1056) with 250 billion cell starter

**Process:**
1. Mash at 153°F (67°C) for 60 minutes
2. Mash out at 168°F (76°C)
3. Collect 7-gallon pre-boil volume
4. 60-minute boil with hop additions per schedule
5. Chill to 65°F (18°C), pitch yeast
6. Ferment at 65°F for 7-9 days
7. Rack to secondary, dry hop for up to 10 days at 68°F
8. Package at 2.5 volumes CO₂ (or 4 oz corn sugar for bottles)

### Irish Dry Stout: Bru'n Dry Stout

*Source: [AHA - Bru'n Dry Stout](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/brun-dry-stout/)*

A classic dry stout emphasizing proper water chemistry for the style's characteristic tart, acidic finish.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5.5 gallons (20.8 L) |
| OG | 1.042 |
| FG | 1.012 |
| ABV | 3.9% |
| IBU | 36 |
| SRM | 31 |
| Efficiency | 76% |

**Grain Bill:**
- 6.5 lb (2.95 kg) Two-row pale malt
- 1.3 lb (590 g) Flaked barley
- 14 oz (397 g) Roast barley

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| East Kent Goldings | 1.5 oz (42 g) | 60 min | 5% |
| East Kent Goldings | 0.5 oz (14 g) | 15 min | 5% |

**Yeast:** Irish ale yeast (White Labs WLP004 or Wyeast 1084)

**Water Chemistry:**
- Use RO or distilled water
- Add ½ tsp gypsum and ½ tsp calcium chloride per 8 gallons
- Acidify to achieve mash pH 5.3-5.5

**Process:**
1. Mash pale malt and flaked barley at 146-150°F for 60 minutes
2. Add roast barley at end of mash (reduces acrid harshness)
3. Optional mash-out to 168°F
4. Sparge with acidified water (pH 5.2-5.7)
5. 60-minute boil
6. Ferment at 64-68°F

**Key tip:** Low-alkalinity water allows the roast barley to provide characteristic tartness without harshness.

### German Hefeweizen: Holier than Meow

*Source: [AHA - Holier than Meow Hefeweizen](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/holier-meow-hefeweizen/)*

Classic Bavarian wheat beer with signature banana and clove character.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (18.9 L) |
| OG | 1.048 |
| FG | 1.010 |
| ABV | 4.9% |
| IBU | 18 |
| SRM | 3.5 |

**Grain Bill:**
- 4.5 lb (2 kg) Pilsner malt
- 4.5 lb (2 kg) Red wheat malt

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| Hallertau | 1.2 oz (34 g) | 60 min | 4% |

**Yeast:** Wyeast 3068 Weihenstephan Weizen OR White Labs WLP300 Hefeweizen
- Make 1.3 qt (1.2 L) starter 2-3 days ahead

**Process:**
1. Strike water ~160°F to mash in at 149°F (65°C)
2. Hold mash 60-75 minutes
3. Mash out at 168°F (76°C)
4. Collect ~5.9 gallons wort
5. 60-75 minute boil
6. **Key fermentation technique:**
   - Chill to 54°F (12°C)
   - Pitch yeast and aerate
   - Allow temperature to rise to 64°F (18°C) during fermentation
   - Rest 3-7 days after fermentation ends
7. Package with high carbonation: 9.5 oz (270 g) corn sugar for 4 volumes CO₂
8. **Use heavy bottles** (Belgian-style) to handle high carbonation

**Flavor notes:** Starting cool and allowing temps to rise produces balanced banana/clove. Starting warm emphasizes banana; cooler temps emphasize clove.

### Bohemian Pilsner: Chodsko Pivo

*Source: [AHA - Chodsko Pivo Bohemian Pilsner](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/chodsko-pivo-bohemian-pilsner-recipe/) - Best of Show winner*

| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |

### Berliner Weisse: Pucker Up Baby

*Source: [AHA - Pucker Up Baby Berliner Weisse](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/pucker-up-baby-berliner-weisse/) by Shane and Jessica Kelley*

A light, refreshing kettle sour with real puckery tartness.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (19 L) |
| OG | 1.030 |
| FG | 1.010 |
| ABV | 3.0% |
| IBU | 6 |
| SRM | 3 |

**Grain Bill:**
- 3.0 lb (1.4 kg) Wheat malt
- 3.0 lb (1.4 kg) Pilsner malt
- 8.0 oz (227 g) Rice hulls (mash aid)
- 1.0 lb (454 g) Uncrushed two-row (for souring — do not mill)

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| Hallertauer | 0.5 oz | 60 min | ~4% |

**Yeast:** Any clean ale yeast (US-05, etc.)

**Kettle Sour Process:**
1. Mash wheat and Pilsner malts at 150°F (66°C) for 60 minutes
2. Collect 5.5 gallons of wort
3. Heat wort to 200°F to sanitize, then cool to 120°F (49°C)
4. Transfer to sanitized keg or insulated cooler
5. Pitch 1 lb uncrushed grain (natural Lactobacillus source)
6. Cover wort surface with plastic wrap to minimize oxygen
7. Maintain 100-120°F for 14-24 hours (taste to check sourness)
8. Strain out grain, bring wort to boil
9. Add hops, boil 60 minutes
10. Chill to 68°F, pitch ale yeast, ferment normally

**Extract Option:** Substitute 3.75 lb wheat DME for grain malts, then follow same souring procedure.

**Key tip:** Longer souring = more tart. Start tasting at 14 hours.

### Fruited Gose: Mangose (Mango Gose)

*Source: [AHA - Mangose](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/mangose/) by Tyler Sadler and Laurie Ann Gutierrez*

A refreshing kettle-soured gose with tropical mango character — sherbet-like tartness.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (18.9 L) |
| OG | 1.048 |
| FG | 1.011 |
| ABV | 4.8% |
| IBU | 8 |
| SRM | 5 |

**Grain Bill:**
- 5 lb (2.3 kg) Pilsner malt
- 2.5 lb (1.1 kg) White wheat malt
- 2.5 lb (1.1 kg) Red wheat malt

**Gose Additions:**
- 0.25 oz (7 g) Crushed coriander seed — add at 10 min
- 0.5 oz (14 g) Sea salt — add at 10 min
- 4 lb (1.8 kg) Frozen mango chunks (thawed) — add to secondary for 5 days

**Hop Schedule:**
| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |
|------|--------|------|-----|
| Saaz | 0.75 oz | 30 min | ~4% |

**Souring Culture:** GoodBelly Mango juice (contains Lactobacillus plantarum)

**Yeast:** 2 packs Fermentis SafAle US-05

**Kettle Sour Process:**
1. Mash at 148°F (64°C) for 75 minutes
2. Sparge normally, collect wort
3. Chill wort to 110°F (43°C)
4. Check pH, add lactic acid if needed to drop to 4.5 (pre-acidification)
5. Pitch Lactobacillus sediment from GoodBelly juice
6. Maintain 90-100°F (32-38°C) for 2 days
7. Target pH: approximately 3.3
8. Bring soured wort to boil
9. Add hops, coriander, and salt per schedule
10. Chill to 70°F, pitch US-05 yeast
11. When fermentation nears completion, add thawed mango to secondary
12. Let sit 5 days on fruit
13. Cold crash 2 days, then package

**Key tip:** The GoodBelly juice provides a reliable, clean Lactobacillus culture. Pre-acidifying to pH 4.5 inhibits unwanted bacteria.

### Traditional Gose: Dollar to Döllnitz

*Source: [AHA - Dollar to Döllnitz Gose](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/dollar-to-dollnitz-gose/) - Homage to Rittergutsbrauerei Döllnitz*

- 10 g Salt

| Hops | Amount | Time | AA% |

### Midwest Fruit Tart (Sherbet-Style Sour Template)

*Source: [AHA - Homebrewing a Midwest Fruit Tart Beer](https://homebrewersassociation.org/how-to-brew/homebrewing-a-midwest-fruit-tart-beer/)*

A template for intensely fruity, sherbet-like sour beers with tropical character.

| Parameter | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons |
| OG | 1.075-1.080 |
| FG | 1.012-1.015 |
| ABV | 7.5-9.0% |
| IBU | 15-25 (bittering only) |
| Fruit | 2.5-3 lb per gallon |
| Final pH | 3.2-3.4 |

**Base Grain Bill Options:**

*Option 1 (The Gadget):*
- 80% Two-row/Pale malt
- 20% Vienna malt

*Option 2 (Fire Iron):*
- 100% Two-row/Pale malt

**Fruit Combinations (examples for 5 gallons):**

*Berry Blend:*
- 7 lb Seedless raspberry puree
- 7 lb Seedless blackberry puree
- 1.5 lb Sugar

*Tropical Blend:*
- 8.75 lb Pink guava puree
- 2.5 lb Passionfruit puree
- 3 lb Banana puree
- 1 lb Sugar

**Kettle Sour Process:**
1. Mash at 148°F (maximize fermentability)
2. Collect only 4 gallons of wort (concentrated)
3. Heat to 180°F to pasteurize
4. Cool to 85°F (29°C)
5. Pitch pure Lactobacillus culture (NOT grain, yogurt, or GoodBelly for this style)
6. Seal vessel, ferment 48-72 hours until pH reaches ~3.4
7. Boil, add bittering hops (no aroma additions)
8. Chill, pitch Kveik yeast

**Fruit Addition Process:**
1. Ferment base beer 2-3 days at 86°F (30°C) with Kveik
2. At high kräusen, add fruit puree, sugar, and yeast nutrients
3. Continue fermentation 7+ days at up to 96°F (35°C)
4. Allow 2 weeks for fruit solids to settle
5. Package at 2.8-3.0 volumes CO₂

**Key tips:**
- Use only 100% fruit purees — no concentrates or flavorings
- Kveik yeast handles high temps and ferments fast
- Optional: 0.1g vanilla per gallon adds depth
- Pink guava and passionfruit are "loud" fruits; use less for subtlety
- The high fruit rate (2.5-3 lb/gal) creates the sherbet-like intensity

### Traditional Dry Mead: Tradition Dry Varietal

*Source: [AHA - Tradition Dry Varietal Mead](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/tradition-dry-varietal-mead/)*

A straightforward traditional mead showcasing honey character.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (19 L) |
| OG | ~1.108 |
| FG | 0.996-1.000 (dry) |
| ABV | 12-15% |

**Ingredients:**
- 15 lb (6.8 kg) Local honey (varietal of choice)
- 4-5 gallons (15-19 L) Water
- 2 packets Lalvin 71B-1122 (or equivalent)
- 0.18 oz (5 g) Fermaid-K (or 2 tsp yeast nutrient + 1 tsp yeast energizer)

**Process:**

### Medieval Mead Recreation (14th Century English)

*Source: [AHA - Boiling & Seething: Recreating Medieval Mead Recipes](https://homebrewersassociation.org/beyond-beer/boiling-seething-recreating-two-medieval-mead-recipes/) from Curye on Inglysch*

Two 14th-century mead recipes with spiced variations.

**Recipe 9: Basic Medieval Mead**

Traditional honey-water fermentation using gentle heating.

**Ingredients:**
- Honeycomb pressings ("pomys")
- "Life honey" (raw, unpressed honey)
- Clean water

**Process:**
1. Press honeycomb to extract "life honey"
2. Simmer comb in water below hand temperature
3. Add life honey (approximately 1 quart per 5 gallons)
4. Let settle in sealed vessel for several days
5. Fermentation begins from ambient yeasts

**Key technique:** "Boyling" and "seething" in medieval texts meant heating below wax's melting point (154°F / 68°C), not actual boiling. This preserved wild yeasts while dissolving honey.

---

**Recipe 10: Fine and Piquant Mead (Spiced)**

A stronger, spiced mead using wort and spices.

**Ingredients (scaled to 5 gallons):**
- 4 gallons pressed honeycomb mead (from Recipe 9)
- 0.6 gallons fine wort (light malt tea)
- 0.2 gallons life honey
- Pepper (to taste)
- Cloves (to taste)

**Process:**
1. Heat all ingredients together until clear
2. Add back to the first mead's vessel for secondary flavoring
3. Age until clear and mellow

**Historical note:** These recipes appear in the earliest known English culinary manuscripts. Previous translations misidentified "pomys" as apples — it actually meant honeycomb pressings.

- 4 gallons (15.1 L) Apple juice (1.050 gravity)
- 1 gallon (3.8 L) Malted barley wort (1.040-1.050 gravity)
- 2 tsp cream of tartar
- 2 twelve-oz cans frozen apple juice concentrate (backsweetening)
- English ale yeast

**Process:**
1. Boil 1 gallon wort tailings for at least 15 minutes
2. Pour apple juice into sanitized 6.5-gallon fermenter with splashing (aeration)
3. Rack wort onto juice
4. Equalize temp to ~68°F, pitch yeast
5. Ferment completely until clear
6. Rack to keg, backsweeten with concentrate to taste
7. Force carbonate, chill to 40°F before serving

**Note:** Not recommended for bottle conditioning with backsweetening (risk of overcarbonation).

## Additional Mead Styles

### Cyser: Take Me Back to Tupelo

*Source: [AHA - Take Me Back to Tupelo Cyser](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/take-me-back-to-tupelo-cyser/)*

A cyser (apple mead) using premium Tupelo honey—sweet, spicy, and worth the investment.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons |
| Style | Cyser (Apple Melomel) |

**Ingredients:**
- 3.5 gallons (13.2 L) Fresh sweet apple juice (chilled)
- 0.5 gallon (1.9 L) Apple juice (for heating)
- 12 lb (5.4 kg) Tupelo honey
- 1 tbsp yeast nutrient
- 2 packets Côte des Blancs yeast

**Process:**
1. Heat ½ gallon juice to 140°F (do NOT boil)
2. Dissolve honey into warm juice
3. Pour chilled juice into sanitized fermenter
4. Add honey mixture and yeast nutrient
5. Pitch yeast, seal fermenter
6. Ferment in dark, cool location (~60°F) for 2-4 weeks
7. Rack when fermentation completes and sediment settles
8. For carbonation: Prime with corn sugar (½ cup in ½ cup boiled water)
9. Bottle with 2 fingers headspace, cap, wait 2 weeks

**Tupelo Honey:** Harvested only in northern Florida and southern Georgia; sweet, spicy character worth its premium price.

### Metheglin: Winter Spiced Mead

*Source: [AHA - Winter Spiced Mead](https://homebrewersassociation.org/homebrew-recipe/winter-spiced-mead/) by Ken Schramm*

A medium-to-sweet still metheglin with layers of complexity that evolve over years of aging.

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Batch Size | 5 gallons (19 L) |
| Style | Metheglin (Spiced Mead) |
| Sweetness | Medium to sweet |
| Carbonation | Still |

**Ingredients:**
- 20 lb (9.07 kg) Honey (raspberry, orange, or blackberry blossom recommended)
- 5+ gallons (19 L) Spring water
- 7 g (1.4 tsp) Ground cinnamon
- 7 g (1.4 tsp) Ground nutmeg
- 7 g (1.4 tsp) Ground dried ginger (optional)
- 1 g (0.2 tsp) Ground cloves (optional)
- 1 small vanilla bean

**Yeast:** Lalvin 71B-1122 or Wyeast 1056 (15g rehydrated)

**Nutrients (TOSNA-style schedule):**
- At lag phase (8-12 hours): 4g DAP + 3g Fermaid K
- Daily for 3 more days: 2g DAP + 2g Fermaid K

**Process:**
1. Mix all ingredients thoroughly
2. Aerate vigorously with mixer or immersion blender
3. Create slightly more than 5 gallons (to fill secondary)
4. Ferment 21-28 days (may be longer with 1056)
5. Transfer to secondary when fermentation completes
6. Age 6-12 months minimum
7. Fine with Sparkolloid if needed
8. Bottle only after all activity ceases

**Ken Schramm's Tip:** This mead will have layers of complexity and will evolve subtly as it matures.
